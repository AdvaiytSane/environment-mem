# Names, instead of "dejado"

12 candidates, three groups of four. No "memo", "mind", "brain", "recall" or "AI" in any of them. Domain notes are from a quick web search each, not a registrar lookup — "looks free" means no product or company turned up using it, not a confirmed available domain.

## Plain English words

The pattern headstart already uses: one word that says "the second try starts ahead," not "memory."

**Carryover** — what the first session leaves for the second, without saying memory. No product in the coding-agent or dev-tool space is using it. .com not checked at a registrar, but nothing prominent is sitting on it.

**Handoff** — names the exact mechanic: one session hands the next its procedure. Taken as a concept: Apple's Handoff is a named Continuity feature people already associate with device-to-device continuity, not agent-to-agent. Confusing to build a second meaning on top of.

**Pickup** — picks up where you left off, one plain word. Crowded: a handful of small apps (a pickup-sports app, two delivery-driver apps) already use it. .com likely gone.

**Foothold** — the next session gets a foothold instead of bare rock. One real conflict, Foothold Technology, a health-record software company, but a different vertical and their domain is footholdtechnology.com, not foothold.com. Plain foothold.com looks open, not confirmed.

## Short invented words

Two syllables, no dictionary meaning tied to memory, easy to say cold.

**Havn** — a respelled "haven," one syllable, a calm word for a base to start from. Crowded: at least five unrelated startups already use Havn, across health, real estate, and a lifestyle app. .com almost certainly gone.

**Loopa** — evokes closing the loop on a task. Used by several small, unrelated companies (a litigation-finance firm, a Brazilian fintech, an AI-agent tool at loopa.im). .com not confirmed but the near-identical .io and .so are already taken by others.

**Contino** — sounds like "continue," short, foreign-brand feel. Taken outright: Contino is an existing multi-million-dollar DevOps consultancy, now part of Cognizant. Drop this one.

**Rello** — evokes "relay." Crowded: a marketing agency, a leasing platform, and a nutrition brand all already use it. .com likely gone, .dev not confirmed.

## Two-word names

**Running Start** — a real track-and-field term for starting already in motion, which is exactly the product. runningstart.dev already belongs to an unrelated tech-careers book, runningstart.org to a nonprofit. .com not confirmed but the phrase isn't unclaimed in tech.

**Second Wind** — implies renewed energy after tiring, which is a weaker fit than it sounds: the product isn't about a tired session getting a boost, it's about not re-doing the first session's work. Also crowded, several small software shops already use the name.

**Prior Art** — a patent term for the existing work that already solved this problem, and the pun is that a recorded procedure is literally that, for a codebase. priorart.dev is already a small personal blog, low traffic, not a company. priorart.com not confirmed, likely open since the phrase is generic outside patent law.

**Carry Forward** — the plain description of what the tool does, no metaphor needed. Generic term already used by tax and workload-automation software (TaxCycle, SAP, HCL), so it carries some accounting-software baggage. carryforward.com not confirmed as taken by any single company, likely open.

## Top three

1. **Carryover** — plainest fit, least conflict found of the twelve, and it reads as a product name rather than a feature name borrowed from someone else's product.
2. **Foothold** — a physical metaphor a judge gets in one word, and the one real conflict sits in a different industry with its own domain already.
3. **Prior Art** — the sharpest pun of the twelve, worth the domain fight since priorart.dev is a low-traffic personal blog, not a company with a claim on the name.
